# What and Why

## What is B.o.B.W.?

B.o.B.W. is a **dual-ingestion boundary layer**. It produces two linked views from the same source:

1. a human-readable record for inspection, context, uncertainty, and decisions; and
2. a machine-readable record for indexing, routing, comparison, and downstream agent use.

The two views are derivatives of one source identity. Neither view silently replaces the source.

## Why a department instead of a loose utility?

The existing Content-Factory already has the later stages: preservation, assay, reusable-material decisions, active-project routing, production, review, and release gates. B.o.B.W. supplies the missing front door for material arriving from multiple tools and formats.

Calling it a department makes its authority and limits visible:

```text
Backlog / source tools
        ↓
B.o.B.W. Customs Agent
        ↓
Traveler-provenance record
        ↓
B.o.B.W. Warehouse Sorter
        ↓
Content-Factory candidate state
        ↓
Assay → component → production → output → Shipping Dock
```

## Why “Best of BOTH Worlds”?

Human-readable material preserves meaning, context, ambiguity, and reviewability. Machine-readable material supports stable identifiers, validation, indexing, relation candidates, and repeatable workflows. B.o.B.W. joins them without flattening either side.

## The operational problem it solves

Accumulated material often exists in disconnected places: browser queues, YouTube transcripts, ChatGPT archive exports, documents, images, and notes. Without a controlled intake boundary, useful material can be duplicated, lose its origin, become impossible to locate, or be mistaken for verified knowledge.

B.o.B.W. converts that backlog into inspectable candidates while keeping the original source and every transformation visible.
